# LEVEL FORMAT — colour-coded image as the map

**Status: decision. Implementation not started.**

This replaces the tile-grid authoring I built in `STAGE-ATLAS.md` Batch A. Two
things drove it:

1. The circuits looked like ovals made of blocks. Blockiness is not a palette
   problem, it is a *representation* problem: a grid of 64-unit cells cannot be
   anything but blocky, and no amount of re-authoring fixes it.
2. Levels will eventually be generated by an AI from text prompts. That makes the
   format itself the load-bearing design decision: it has to be something a model
   can emit reliably, and the most reliable thing a model can emit is an image.

So: **a circuit is a colour-coded image.** Each colour names a surface. That
image is the map. Everything else is compiled from it.

---

## The constraint that decides the resolution

A per-pixel surface map does not fit. Largest circuit is 64x54 tiles = 4096x3456
world units:

| Map resolution | 4-bit indexed, one circuit | all 24 |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| per world pixel | 6.75 MB | 162 MB |
| 4 px cells | 432 KB | 10.4 MB |
| 16 px cells | 27 KB | 648 KB |
| 64 px cells (today's tiles) | 1.7 KB | 40 KB |

The machine has **2 MB total, 999 KB budgeted, 366 KB free**. So the *physics* map
cannot be per-pixel and cannot even be 16 px cells across 24 circuits.

The reconciliation, and it is the whole trick:

> **Author per pixel. Compile coarse. Render per pixel.**

- The **authoring image** can be any resolution an AI likes -- 256x256, 512x512,
  high detail. It is a build input, not shipped data.
- The **physics map** is downsampled to 64 px cells (40 KB for all 24). It only
  needs to answer "what surface is under the car", and a cell is finer than the
  car.
- The **visual** is a baked texture plus per-pixel geometry, so the *rendering*
  is per-pixel accurate even though the collision map is coarse.

This is why "per pixel" and "fits in 2 MB" are not in conflict: they apply to
different things. Getting this wrong is what made the first attempt look blocky --
the render was reading the same coarse grid the physics used.

**Streaming (Batch D) becomes mandatory, not optional.** One circuit's baked
texture resident at a time, swapped on load. With 366 KB free there is no room for
24 resident textures.

## The palette

16 entries, 4-bit indexed, fixed. These are the codes an AI writes.

| # | RGB | Name | Grip | Top speed | Drivable | Notes |
| ---: | :--- | :--- | --- | --- | :--- | :--- |
| 0 | `#000000` | `VOID` | - | - | no | outside the circuit; never referenced |
| 1 | `#3C3E44` | `TARMAC` | 1.00 | 1.00 | yes | racing surface |
| 2 | `#4A4C54` | `TARMAC_WORN` | 0.98 | 1.00 | yes | subtle variation, for lap lines |
| 3 | `#E8E8F0` | `KERB_WHITE` | 0.90 | 0.98 | yes | rumble; also audio + haptic |
| 4 | `#D8283C` | `KERB_RED` | 0.90 | 0.98 | yes | rumble, alternating with 3 |
| 5 | `#2E5A34` | `GRASS` | 0.55 | 0.70 | yes | runoff |
| 6 | `#6B5432` | `GRAVEL` | 0.42 | 0.55 | yes | runoff, slower |
| 7 | `#7A6090` | `SAND` | 0.35 | 0.45 | yes | runoff, slowest |
| 8 | `#8A1FB0` | `OIL` | 0.02 | 1.00 | yes | near-zero lateral grip |
| 9 | `#FF8A10` | `BOOST` | 1.00 | 1.00 | yes | instant impulse along heading |
| 10 | `#F2F2F2` | `START_LINE` | 1.00 | 1.00 | yes | start/finish, also a lap gate |
| 11 | `#00D2FF` | `GATE` | 1.00 | 1.00 | yes | checkpoint gate |
| 12 | `#2A2E38` | `WALL` | - | - | no | solid; blocks the car |
| 13 | `#5A4632` | `TUNNEL` | 1.00 | 1.00 | yes | tarmac + roof (Batch E) |
| 14 | `#3A5A7A` | `BRIDGE` | 1.00 | 1.00 | yes | tarmac, crosses below (Batch E) |
| 15 | `#FF2E88` | `SCENERY` | 0.50 | 0.60 | yes | palm/rock/fence/spectator base |

Rules an authoring image must satisfy, all checked by the compiler:

- **Closed circuit.** `TARMAC` (1) must form exactly one connected ring, and it
  must be a ring: a spur or a dead end is a validation failure.
- **Road width.** The drivable ring is **2 to 3 cells wide** at 64 px cells.
  Wider reads as a car park and makes corners meaningless.
- **Every drivable cell must reach every other.** No isolated islands.
- **Every `TARMAC` cell touches `WALL`, or the runoff, within 6 cells.** A track
  edge with nothing beyond it is an invisible wall at the image border.
- **Kerbs alternate.** `KERB_WHITE`/`KERB_RED` must alternate along an edge, or
  the rumble reads as a solid stripe.
- **Hazards sit on road.** `BOOST`, `OIL`, `GATE`, `START_LINE` must be surrounded
  by `TARMAC`, or they are unreachable and unscoreable.
- **`VOID` is forbidden** inside the circuit's bounding box.

Violations are hard errors with the offending cell coordinates. An AI iterating
against a compiler that names the broken cell is a workable loop; one guessing at
a silent failure is not.

## What the compiler produces

`tools/track_cook/compile_circuit.py`, one circuit per image:

| Output | Format | Size | Used by |
| :--- | :--- | ---: | :--- |
| surface grid | packed 4-bit, 64 px cells | ~1.7 KB | physics, AI, minimap basis |
| baked texture | 4-bit CLUT + 8x8 cells, streamed | ~30-60 KB | visuals |
| centreline | control points, tile coords | ~200 B | `Route`, camera, lap validation |
| gates | arc positions along the centreline | ~50 B | `CheckpointGate` (step 3) |

The centreline is derived from the image by skeletonising the `TARMAC` ring and
resampling it -- **not** authored by the AI, and not hand-placed. It has to agree
with the image by construction or step 4's lap validation fails.

## Consequences for work already done

Stated plainly, because some of it gets thrown away:

- **Batch A (authored superellipse circuits) is superseded.** The circuits are now
  authored as images. What survives is the *validation* work -- the cusp check,
  the closure check, and the wide-road/long-straight measurements are all still
  wanted, applied to the compiled geometry instead.
- **Batch B / step 5 (ribbon rendering) is promoted from "nice to have" to the
  core of this.** Per-pixel accuracy *is* the ribbon. The tile blitter
  (`tile_blitter.rs`) goes away; it is the source of the blockiness.
- **The texture pipeline built in `343f7dd` is now load-bearing** rather than an
  optimisation: it is how the baked visual gets to VRAM.
- **Step 3's segment gates become mandatory**, because a gate derived from an
  image cannot be a 1x1 tile. Already known: a square gate's corners land in the
  runoff.
- **`MAX_TRACK_DIM` 40 -> 64 stays.** Larger circuits are wanted.

## Order of work

1. Palette + authoring-image spec **as a machine-readable file** (so an AI is
   pointed at data, not prose).
2. Compiler: image -> surface grid + centreline, with the validation rules above.
3. Replace the tile blitter with per-pixel geometry rendering. *This is the step
   that removes the blockiness; nothing before it will look right.*
4. Bake the visual texture; stream one circuit at a time.
5. Segment gates + arc-crossing lap validation.
6. Scenery props, on the `SCENERY` code.

Steps 1-2 are small and unblock everything. Step 3 is the one that matters and
the one with no shortcut.
